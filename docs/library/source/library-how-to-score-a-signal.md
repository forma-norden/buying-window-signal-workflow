# How to Score a Signal

> **What it targets:** Any account-level or contact-level event before you decide whether to activate outbound, ABM, LinkedIn warming, or monitoring.

## The logic

Do not trigger outreach from the signal alone.

Score it first.

That discipline matters because most GTM systems are weaker than they look. **76%** of CRM users report that less than half of their data is accurate and complete, and **37%** say poor CRM data quality is already costing revenue (Validity, July 2025). If the account record is weak, the signal will look stronger than it really is.

## The scorecard

| Filter | Question | Score |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ICP fit | Is this account actually inside our target vertical, size, stage, and deal motion? | 0-3 |
| Timing strength | Does the event suggest active evaluation, budget movement, or operational change? | 0-3 |
| Contactability | Can we identify and reach the right role with verified data? | 0-2 |
| Route clarity | Do we know whether this belongs in outbound, ABM, LinkedIn, or monitoring? | 0-2 |

## Interpretation

> - `0-3` -> Ignore or monitor
> - `4-6` -> Enrich first, then re-score
> - `7-8` -> Activate a light motion
> - `9-10` -> Activate immediately

## Route rules

| Score | Default route | What to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0-3 | Ignore / monitor | Forcing outreach just because the signal looks interesting |
| 4-6 | Enrich + watch | Writing copy before data and ownership are clear |
| 7-8 | Triggered outbound or LinkedIn | Escalating into full ABM too early |
| 9-10 | Full triggered motion | Waiting 3-5 days and losing the timing advantage |

## Quick check before you send

> Ask four questions:
>
> 1. Did something actually change?
> 2. Is the account still in our lane?
> 3. Can we reach the right person fast?
> 4. Do we know the correct first motion?

If any answer is no, do not send yet.

## Common mistake

Most teams treat all signals like direct send triggers.

They are not.

A pricing-page revisit from a perfect-fit account with verified contacts is not the same as a loose hiring clue from a weak-fit company. The scorecard exists to stop those two things from being treated as equal.
